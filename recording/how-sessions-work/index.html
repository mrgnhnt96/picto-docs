How Sessions Work

How a session splits into segments, what happens when the app goes to the background or is killed, and how uploads are queued, retried and rate limited.

A session is one visit to your app, and it reaches the backend as one or more segments. Each segment is a complete recording, uploaded in its own request when it ends. Picto ends and uploads segments on its own, so once a session is recording you don't need to call anything else.

Sessions and segments#

Every upload carries a session_id and a sequence. The session_id is shared by every segment of a session, and sequence counts the segments from 0. A session that ended without being split has one segment, with sequence 0.

Segments are ordered by sequence, not by arrival. A segment that waited in the pending-upload queue for a day still sorts into its place.

A session counts once toward your plan however many segments it has. See Plans & Retention.

What ends a segment#

These end the live segment and upload it:

TriggerWhat follows
The app goes to the background The same session continues if the app returns within backgroundTimeout . See Going to the background .
The segment reaches maxRecordingDuration (10 minutes by default) The next segment of the same session starts straight away.
Your endSessionAndUpload() or restartRecording() See Recording Sessions.
The error capture window closes Picto goes back to its error lookback buffer. See Errors.
The app exits The segment is saved to the queue and sent at the next launch. See When the app is killed .

A segment that continues the same session is a new recording. Its clock starts at zero, and its hadError describes only that segment. Tags set with Picto.setTag don't carry into it; the user named by Picto.identify does. See Adding Context.

Going to the background#

When the app goes to the background, Picto ends the live segment and starts uploading it at once. It doesn't hold the recording in memory in case the user comes back, because the OS can kill a backgrounded app at any moment.

What backgroundTimeout decides is the session id. It's 30 seconds by default, and Picto checks it when the app returns:

Time awayThe next segment is
Less than backgroundTimeout The same session, at the next sequence
backgroundTimeout or longer A new session, at sequence 0

To change it, pass backgroundTimeout to Picto.setup:

Picto.setup(
  apiKey: const String.fromEnvironment('PICTO_API_KEY'),
  appVersion: '1.0.0',
  backgroundTimeout: const Duration(minutes: 2),
);

A negative value is treated as Duration.zero, which makes every return start a new session. The same window decides whether your own end followed by a quick startRecording() continues a session. A new session started on return counts toward maxNewSessionsPerHour.

On macOS and the web, a window that is minimized, hidden or fully covered, and a browser tab the user switched away from, counts as the background. A window that only loses focus keeps recording.

When recording doesn't resume#

Picto starts recording again on return only for a session it ended itself, in an app that called startRecording(). In these cases it doesn't:

  • You ended the session. Your endSessionAndUpload() means stop. Call startRecording() to begin again.
  • You never called startRecording(). The only session such an app has is one an uncaught error started, and that error buys one session. On return, Picto runs only its error lookback buffer.
  • The session had recorded an uncaught error. Its id is retired. If you called startRecording(), the return starts a new session at sequence 0.
  • Sampling declined this run. Picto checks the sampling decision again on return. See Sampling.
  • The app was killed while away. The next launch starts fresh. The segment from before the kill was already uploaded or queued.

When the app is killed#

Picto saves the live segment so a kill without warning loses as little as possible:

  • On inactive, such as when the iOS app switcher opens, Picto writes a checkpoint of the live segment to disk. It doesn't end the segment, because inactive is also the notification shade and Control Center.
  • Every 30 seconds while a segment is recording something new, Picto rewrites the checkpoint. This covers exits with no lifecycle event at all, such as an iPad app in a resized window.

If the process dies, the next launch finds the checkpoint and sends it under its original session_id and sequence. A kill with no warning loses at most about the last 30 seconds. When the app returns from inactive the checkpoint is deleted, so a kill in the 30 seconds after that loses the segment.

Some exits wait for the segment to be saved:

  • Android Back on the root route closes the app. While a session is recording, Picto holds that exit until the segment is in the queue, for at most 2 seconds. Your PopScopes and routes handle Back first, as usual. In an app that opts into predictive back, Picto keeps Back with Flutter while recording so the exit goes through this hold. The app then loses the back-to-home animation for that exit, and the next launch is a cold start.
  • Quitting a macOS app (Cmd+Q, or closing the last window) is held the same way, for at most 2 seconds, after your own exit handlers agree to quit.
  • A web tab that closes or reloads saves the segment in the browser. The next visit sends it. A small segment is also sent as the page unloads.

A segment saved on exit is sent at the next launch, not right away.

Uploading a segment#

A segment is uploaded whole, in one request. Before the first attempt, Picto writes it to the pending-upload queue on disk, and it leaves the queue only when the backend accepts it. A process that dies mid-upload doesn't lose it.

Exceptions, slow frames and tap clusters from the segment are sent after the recording is accepted, never before. If the recording is queued, they're queued with it.

Retries and timeouts#

Picto makes up to 4 attempts, waiting 1, 4 and 16 seconds between them. Whether it tries again depends on the response:

ResponseWhat Picto does
No response (offline, DNS or TLS failure, timeout) or 503 Retries. After the last attempt, the segment stays queued.
429 (rate limited) Stays queued without retrying. See Rate limits.
400 (the recording can never be stored, such as empty data) Drops it and says so on the console. The same bytes would be refused again.
403 or any other status Doesn't retry. The segment stays queued and the console names the status. For 403 , see Troubleshooting .

Each attempt waits up to 120 seconds, plus time to send the body at 2,000 bytes per second, so a large segment on a slow link isn't cut off.

Background upload time#

Android and iOS cut off a backgrounded app's network within seconds, which a slow connection or a long segment won't survive. While the upload a backgrounding started is running, Picto's plugin keeps the app able to send it:

  • Android 12 and later: an expedited job. If the system tears down the Flutter engine while the process lives, the plugin sends the queue itself.
  • iOS: a background task, which iOS allows for about 30 seconds. An upload still running when it expires is handed to a background URLSession, which iOS finishes even while the app is suspended.

Neither asks the user for a permission or shows anything. The OS can still stop either early. An upload that doesn't finish stays queued. On the web and macOS the app keeps running in the background, so nothing extra is needed.

The pending-upload queue#

A segment that isn't accepted stays in the queue and is sent later. The queue holds at most 50 entries and 100 MB; past that, the oldest entries are removed. The newest entry is always kept.

By default the queue lives in a private directory that survives app updates: no_backup/ on Android and Library/Application Support/ on iOS and macOS. On the web it's in the browser's IndexedDB. To choose the directory, pass pendingUploadsDir to Picto.setup, and pick one that also survives updates.

Each entry stores everything needed to send it again, except the API key: a queued segment is sent with the key the app is running with now.

Draining the queue#

Picto sends the queue on its own:

  • once at Picto.setup;
  • each time the app returns to the foreground.

Both are limited to one drain every 5 minutes. The exception is a segment that was queued because the backend couldn't be reached: the next return drains it even inside the 5 minutes. A drain sends the oldest entries first and stops at the first 429, leaving the rest for the next drain.

You don't need to call anything. To drain at a moment you know is better, such as when your own connectivity check reports the device is back online, call retryPendingUploads(): 

import 'dart:async';

import 'package:picto/picto.dart';

void onBackOnline() {
  unawaited(Picto.retryPendingUploads());
}

A call made while a drain is running joins it instead of starting a second one, and it counts toward the 5-minute limit on automatic drains. It throws a StateError if Picto.setup was never called, and does nothing under a test binding.

Rate limits#

The backend limits uploads per device and per app, in fixed 5-minute windows. A window starts at the first request and resets 5 minutes later, so a 429 clears within minutes, not seconds.

Each device can send, per 5 minutes:

UploadLimit
Recordings (segments)20
Exceptions60
Rage-tap clusters60
Slow-frame events120

Each app has a larger budget for recordings that depends on its plan, per 5 minutes:

PlanRecordings per app
Starter1,000
Growth5,000
Scale20,000
Enterprise50,000

The app budget for exceptions and rage-tap clusters is three times the recording budget, and for slow-frame events six times.

A device that starts many short sessions in a burst, such as a test device looping a flow, reaches the per-device limit first. Its segments stay queued and arrive a few minutes later. Nothing is lost.
